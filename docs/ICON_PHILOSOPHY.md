# Gyllen Mørke — a design philosophy

*(Golden Dark)*

## The movement

Gyllen Mørke is the aesthetics of what waits. It is the visual language of
objects kept deliberately in darkness so that they may become more than they
were — the cellar, the vault, the archive. Nothing in this movement is bright;
light exists only as a promise, a thin line of gold discovered by a candle.
The canvas is never black. It is *deep* — a darkness with temperature, tuned
toward wine and old brick, so the eye senses warmth before it senses form.

## Space and form

Form is singular and ceremonial. One object, centered, held in generous
darkness — the way a single bottle is held up to the light. Silhouettes are
distilled until they read at any distance: a curve that is unmistakably a
bowl, a stem that is unmistakably a stem. Ornament is forbidden; proportion
is the only decoration permitted. Every arc must look drawn by a hand that
has drawn it ten thousand times — the confident economy of a master
glassblower's gesture, meticulously refined until nothing can be removed.

## Color and material

Two darknesses and one metal. The first darkness is burgundy — layered,
radial, breathing, darker at the edges like vaulted stone. The second is
graphite: a cooler, flatter black that sits *inside* the first, the way a
record lies in its sleeve. It is never announced by a hard edge of its own;
it is felt as a change of temperature, a circle where the warmth goes still.
The gold is not flat; it carries the gradient of candlelight on metal,
brighter at the crown, burnished at the base. Gold is spent like currency:
a thin ring, a surface line, a silhouette — never a flood. Where gold meets
either darkness, the edge is crisp; craftsmanship lives in that edge.

## Scale and rhythm

The work must survive miniaturization. Composed at monument scale, judged at
the scale of a thumbnail — the discipline of the engraver, whose work is
measured in millimeters but labored over for weeks. Weight over detail;
geometry over texture. A quiet secondary rhythm may echo the primary form —
an arch behind a glass, a ring behind a crown — at a fraction of the opacity,
felt rather than seen. Where that rhythm repeats — groove after groove,
each a hair's width, each fainter than the last — it is laid down with the
patience of an engraver: the accumulation should read as one surface at
arm's length and as countless marks up close.

## The reference, kept quiet

The subject is never announced. It is embedded: the vault of a cellar closed
into the ring of a record, the surface of a poured glass in a single
horizontal line, the grooves as a whisper behind the stem, patience itself
in the stillness of the composition. Those who keep a cellar — or a shelf of
records — will recognize it intuitively; everyone else simply sees a
well-made object.
This restraint is non-negotiable — the mark of work produced by someone at
the absolute top of their field, with painstaking attention to what is left
out.

## Applied: the app icon (Fase 7)

Source: `public/icons/icon.svg` (regular) and `icon-maskable.svg` (same
composition at 86 % so the ring stays inside the 80 % safe zone). Rasters are
drawn from these — never edited by hand: 512, 192, 180 (apple-touch), 64
(favicon), 512 maskable.

- **Burgundy darkness** — the cellar, unchanged from Vinkjeller.
- **Graphite darkness** — the record, a 208 px disc: a cooler radial black
  with sixteen grooves at 7 % gold and three light-catching ones at 20 %.
  Its only hard edge is a 5 px gold ring, the vault closed into a circle.
- **Gold** — the glass, scaled 1.12 over the earlier icon, wine poured to
  one line. A dark halo under every stroke keeps the edge crisp where the
  bowl and the foot cross the grooves.
- The record's hole sits behind the stem. It is there; it is not shown.
